BILL HWANG

FOUNDER • ARCHÉGOS CAPITAL MANAGEMENT

From Tiger Cub to Covert Leverage

Formerly of Julian Robertson's renowned "Tiger Cub" lineage at Tiger Asia Management, Hwang established the Archegos family office in 2013 with an initial core of personal capital, managing approximately $10 billion by 2020. The firm took its title from the Greek word αρχηγός (archegos), signifying "leader" or "pioneer."

Regulatory Disqualification & The Family Office Pivot

Tiger Asia Management had previously pleaded guilty in 2012 to federal insider trading charges involving Chinese bank stocks, settling with the SEC and DOJ for $44 million in fines. In 2014, Hong Kong regulatory authorities banned Hwang from trading in Hong Kong securities for four years. Unable to manage external client funds under standard investment advisory regulations, Hwang transitioned his wealth into a private family office.

By converting to a private family office, Archegos was exempt under the Dodd-Frank Act from registering with the SEC as an investment adviser and exempt from publicly disclosing its portfolio holdings quarterly on SEC Form 13F. This regulatory boundary permitted Hwang to secretly assemble enormous synthetic equity positions across multiple global investment banks.

DOSSIER CHAPTER 03 • CORPORATE STRUCTURE

The Vehicle: A Blind Spot by Design

Unlike traditional hedge funds that manage external institutional capital, Archegos operated as a private single-family office, legally shielded from mandatory public transparency.

The Family Office Exemption

Under Rule 202(a)(11)(G)-1 of the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, family offices managing exclusively the wealth of "family clients" are excluded from the definition of an investment adviser. Consequently, Archegos was never required to:

EXEMPTION 01 File Form ADV detailing assets under management, risk controls, and executive disciplinary history.
EXEMPTION 02 Submit quarterly SEC Form 13F filings disclosing beneficial equity holdings over $100 million.
EXEMPTION 03 Submit to routine examination or regulatory stress-testing by the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Scale and Capital Velocity

Managing $10 billion in equity as of late 2020, Archegos experienced explosive growth as Hwang channeled synthetic derivative profits back into concentrated equity bets. At its apex in early 2021, the family office controlled over $36 billion in net capital.

However, this $36 billion base was merely the collateral wedge. Deployed at 5x to 8x leverage through bilateral swap facilities across Wall Street, Archegos commanded effective economic exposure exceeding $100 billion in publicly traded companies.

DOSSIER CHAPTER 04 • FINANCIAL ARCHITECTURE

Total Return Swaps: The Synthetic Veil

Archegos's holdings were primarily structured as bilateral Total Return Swaps (TRS). This derivative mechanism enabled the firm to capture the complete economic upside and downside of common stocks while the lending banks held the legal title to the shares.

FAMILY OFFICE BUY-SIDE

Archegos Capital

Posts initial margin collateral (10%–15%). Receives total capital appreciation and dividend yields on underlying shares.

Public Disclosures: NONE REQUIRED (13F EXEMPT)
BILATERAL TOTAL RETURN SWAP SYNTHETIC DERIVATIVE

Transfers economic returns and losses between Archegos and prime broker. No shares change hands.

EQUITY HEDGING TRANSACTIONS PHYSICAL PURCHASE

Prime broker buys common shares on open exchange in its own corporate name to offset swap liability.

PRIME BROKER SELL-SIDE

Global Investment Banks

Credit Suisse, Nomura, Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs, UBS. Holds physical shares on balance sheet.

Registered Legal Owner: PRIME BROKER BANK

Archegos executes a bilateral Total Return Swap (TRS) with a prime broker bank. Archegos posts a modest margin and receives economic returns without direct share ownership.

FAST EXIT • MITIGATION

Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley & Deutsche Bank

Recognizing that Hwang could not meet margin calls, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley commenced aggressive block sales early on Friday, March 26. Deutsche Bank acted swiftly, dumping approximately $4 billion in positions to avoid any material loss. Morgan Stanley absorbed a $911 million loss ($644M on sales and $267M derisking).

Goldman Sachs ($0) Deutsche Bank ($0) Morgan Stanley (-$911M)
DELAYED EXIT • CATASTROPHIC HIT

Credit Suisse & Nomura Holdings

Credit Suisse and Nomura hesitated through the weekend of March 27–28, hoping to negotiate an orderly, coordinated liquidation among lenders. When talks collapsed and competitors sold off underlying stocks, Credit Suisse and Nomura were forced to liquidate into a cratering market on Monday and Tuesday, incurring historic losses of $5.5B and $2.85B.

DOSSIER CHAPTER 12 • JURISDICTION & PROSECUTION

The Legal Dossier: Allegations vs Findings

Preserving strict legal distinctions between prosecutorial allegations, defense claims, regulatory enforcement actions, jury verdicts, and final judicial sentences.

PROSECUTORIAL ALLEGATION

Stock Price Manipulation Scheme

Federal prosecutors alleged that Hwang and CFO Patrick Halligan schemed to artificially manipulate stock prices by placing coordinated pre-market and market-on-close bids, creating false trading volume to drive up swap valuations and induce banks into granting further credit.

Source: 59-Page Indictment U.S. DOJ (SDNY)
CRIMINAL INDICTMENT • APRIL 2022

RICO, Securities Fraud & Wire Fraud

On April 27, 2022, federal agents arrested Bill Hwang and former CFO Patrick Halligan. The indictment charged racketeering conspiracy under the RICO Act, securities fraud, and wire fraud in connection with Archegos's multibillion-dollar implosion.

Judge: Alvin K. Hellerstein Case: 22-CR-240
DEFENSE POSITION

Claims of Innocence & Strategy

Attorneys for Hwang and Halligan maintained that their clients were completely innocent of all criminal charges, arguing that Hwang pursued legitimate investment beliefs in target companies and that prime broker banks were sophisticated market institutions fully aware of the derivative risks.

Filings: Dec 2022 Defense Counsel
JURY CONVICTION • JULY 2024

Guilty on 10 of 11 Criminal Counts

Following a multi-week trial in Manhattan federal court, a jury convicted Sung Kook Hwang on 10 of 11 criminal counts, finding him guilty of fraud, racketeering conspiracy, and market manipulation that triggered over $100 billion in shareholder market losses.

Verdict: July 2024 Manhattan Federal Jury
FINAL SENTENCE • NOV 21, 2024

18 Years in Federal Prison

U.S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein sentenced Bill Hwang to 18 years in federal prison for orchestrating the massive fraud. Judge Hellerstein characterized the collapse as a catastrophic loss of institutional integrity.

Sentence: 18 Years Prison Reuters (Cohen & Stempel)
REGULATORY PENALTY • JULY 2023

$387M Fine Against Credit Suisse

In July 2023, Credit Suisse (having been acquired in an emergency transaction by UBS) was fined $387 million by the U.S. Federal Reserve and British Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) for egregious risk management failures regarding Archegos.

Regulators: Fed & PRA Penalty: $387 Million
